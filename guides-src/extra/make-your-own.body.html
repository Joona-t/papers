Make your own study guide with AI

The guides in this archive were written for a general reader. An AI model such as Claude can make one shaped around you instead: your background, your goal, the format you learn best from. This page shows how to ask, and how to check what you get back.

Why make your own?

A ready-made guide has to guess who is reading. Yours doesn't. You can ask for explanations that start from what you already know ("I'm a nurse", "I know Python but no calculus"), analogies from things you care about, more depth on the part that confused you, and a format that suits how you learn. You can also keep asking follow-up questions, which no static page can answer.

What to put in your request

  1. The paper. Paste the link from this archive, or upload the PDF. Ask the AI to read the paper itself, not to work from memory.
  2. Who you are. What you already know, and what you don't. "Assume no maths beyond school algebra" or "I've trained small neural nets before" changes the whole guide.
  3. Why you're reading it. General curiosity, an exam, a job interview, building something with the idea, or deciding whether a news story is right.
  4. The format. See the list below. You can ask for more than one.
  5. How to keep it honest. Ask for section or page references for every key claim, and for the AI to say plainly when it is unsure or when a later paper changed the idea.

Check what you get

AI models can be confidently wrong, especially about exact numbers, who invented what, and papers newer than their training. A few habits catch most problems:

  • Spot-check the references. Open the paper at two or three of the cited sections and confirm they say what the guide claims.
  • Be wary of precise numbers. Benchmark scores, dataset sizes and hyperparameters are where errors hide. Find them in the paper's tables.
  • Get a second opinion. Paste the guide into a fresh chat (or a different AI) with the last prompt above and ask it to fact-check.
  • Read the abstract and conclusion yourself. If the guide's headline doesn't match them, trust the paper.
  • Ask "what came after?". Many ideas in this archive were later refined. The archive's "Led to" links are a good place to look.

Rule of thumbUse the AI to understand the paper, and the paper to check the AI.
